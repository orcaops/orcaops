"Orcaops", the orca mark and the Orcaops wordmark are unregistered trademarks
of Orcaops Inc. The [LICENSE](LICENSE) covers the software and says
plainly that it grants no rights in them. This document says what you may do
with the name anyway.

The mark files themselves — everything under `apps/docs/content/public/` — are
excluded from the CC BY 4.0 carve-out the LICENSE grants over documentation
text. They stay under the Functional Source License, so copying or modifying
them is a licence question as well as a trademark one.

## Nominative use — always fine

You do not need permission to use the name truthfully to refer to this
project. That includes:

- Saying your tool works with, integrates with, is built on, or is compatible
  with orcaops.
- Naming orcaops in documentation, tutorials, blog posts, talks, comparisons,
  reviews, and course material — favourable or not.
- Using the name in the body of a package description, a README, or release
  notes to say what your software does.
- Linking to the project, its documentation, or its npm package.

The rule behind all of those: use the name to **describe**, not to **brand**.
Use no more of the mark than you need, and do not use the logo or our visual
styling in a way that suggests we made or endorsed your thing.

## What a fork may not do

Forking is permitted by the license and is a legitimate response to a change
we did not make. A fork may say it is a fork of orcaops. A fork may not
present itself as orcaops. Concretely, do not:

- **Publish under a name a user could mistake for ours.** No package, binary,
  or product named `orcaops`, `@orcaops/…`, or a near-miss like `orca-ops`,
  `orcaops-cli`, `orcaops-plus`, or `Orcaops Community Edition`. Pick your own
  name and say "a fork of orcaops" underneath it.
- **Reuse our logo, wordmark, colours, or site design** as the identity of
  your build.
- **Use our domains or handles**, or register domains, social accounts, or
  package scopes that read as official.
- **Call your build official, certified, supported, endorsed, or upstream** —
  or imply it by omission. If a reasonable user would have to read carefully
  to work out that we did not publish it, the labelling is wrong.
- **Attribute your changes to us.** State clearly that your build is modified
  and that bugs in it are yours. Do not point your users at our issue tracker
  or our security reporting channel for defects in your fork.
- **Present modified output as ours.** If your fork changes what orcaops
  reports — evaluator verdicts, captured artifacts, review output — do not let
  that output claim to be produced by orcaops.

## Redistribution without modification

Packaging orcaops unmodified — a distro package, a container image, an
internal mirror — may use the name to say what it contains. Say which version
you packaged, and where you got it. Do not imply that we maintain the
packaging.

## When in doubt

Ask. Open an issue describing the use — that is the channel that reaches us,
and the one we would much rather use than any of the alternatives.
